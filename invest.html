Invest in Morocco — Africa's factory door to Europe

Morocco combines free-trade access to large markets, a top Mediterranean port, a proven automotive base and a fast-growing Chinese industrial community.

The investment case

Six reasons Chinese companies choose Morocco

🌍

Market access

Free-trade agreements with the EU, the United States and many African and Arab states make Morocco a manufacturing base for multiple markets.

⚓

Tanger Med

One of the Mediterranean's largest container ports, on the Strait of Gibraltar, with adjacent industrial and logistics zones.

🚗

Automotive base

Renault and Stellantis plants anchor a deep supplier network; passenger-vehicle exports reached about $6.4B in a recent year.

🔋

EV & battery cluster

Announced Chinese projects include Gotion ($1.3B, 20 GWh), CNGR (~$2B) and BTR ($300M), plus phosphate and cobalt resources.

🏙️

Tangier Tech City

42 enterprise agreements, including 34 Chinese firms, with about $3.5B of planned investment (Xinhua, 2026).

🤝

Political ties

Morocco was the first North African country to join the Belt and Road Initiative, and China's zero-tariff policy for African partners took effect in 2026.

Roadmap

From idea to operating company: 7 steps

  1. Feasibility & site visit — shortlist 2–3 locations, meet zone operators and your regional investment centre (CRI).
  2. Choose a legal form — most foreign investors use an SARL (limited liability) or SA (joint-stock) company, or open a branch.
  3. Reserve the name — obtain the negative certificate from OMPIC.
  4. Draft statutes & deposit capital — with a notary or lawyer; open a bank account.
  5. Register — trade register, tax ID, ICE business identifier and social security (CNSS), often through the CRI one-stop shop.
  6. Zone licence & incentives — apply for zone admission or an investment agreement where relevant.
  7. Hire & operate — work permits for foreign staff, local hiring, customs procedures.
Pro move: run steps 2–5 in parallel with a local law firm. Ask for a fixed-fee quote covering all registrations.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreign company own 100% of a Moroccan company?

In most sectors, yes. Foreign investors can generally hold 100% of a Moroccan company; a few regulated activities have specific rules. Confirm your sector with the regional investment centre (CRI) or a local lawyer.

What is an industrial acceleration zone?

It is Morocco's current name for its export-oriented free zones (e.g. around Tangier, Kenitra and other regions). They offer customs and tax advantages for qualifying activities. Exact incentives change with finance laws, so verify current terms before deciding.

Where do Chinese battery companies invest in Morocco?

Announced projects include Gotion's gigafactory near Kenitra, CNGR's materials base (with Al Mada) and BTR's cathode plant, alongside the Mohammed VI Tangier Tech City.
