Importing from China to Morocco: step by step

By the 00212 Editorial Desk · Updated 2026-09-28 · 7 min read

China is Morocco's largest trading partner in Asia. In 2025 it exported about $9.9B of goods to Morocco. Here is the process professional importers follow.

1. Find and verify the supplier

  • Check the business licence (营业执照) and export history.
  • Order samples and, for large orders, a factory audit.
  • Pay a deposit (commonly 30%) and the balance after inspection. Avoid paying 100% upfront.

2. Agree the Incoterm

FOB (you control the freight) and CIF (the supplier ships to your port) are the most common. Moroccan customs values goods on a CIF basis.

3. Classify the goods (HS code)

The HS code sets the import duty rate, any licensing or standards requirements, and eligibility for preferences. A wrong code is the #1 cause of penalties.

4. Documents

  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of lading or air waybill
  • Certificate of origin
  • Conformity certificates where required (e.g. electrical products, toys)
  • Importer registration (ICE) and customs declaration through a licensed broker

5. Duties & taxes

Expect import duty (it depends on the HS code; non-preferential rates for many consumer goods are high), a 0.25% parafiscal import tax, and import VAT (standard 20%) calculated on the duty-paid value. Estimate your landed cost →

6. Shipping & clearance

Sea freight to Casablanca or Tanger Med typically takes 30–45 days, often with transshipment. Air freight to Casablanca takes about 3–7 days. Brokers file the declaration electronically, and inspections can add days, so accurate paperwork is the best way to speed things up.

Checklist before you pay the balance: inspection report OK · HS code confirmed by broker · certificate of origin ready · labels meet Moroccan rules (French/Arabic where required).
